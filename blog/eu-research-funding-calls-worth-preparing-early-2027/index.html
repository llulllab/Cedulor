EU Research Funding Calls Worth Preparing For

By the Cedulor team. As of 11 October 2026.

The calls below span doctoral training, social innovation, deep-tech company scaling, territorial mission deployment, and civilian-military cybersecurity, reflecting the breadth of European research and innovation funding. Reviewing each call's scope and expected outcomes carefully will help research offices and applicants position their work with precision well before any deadline.

MSCA Doctoral Networks 2026

Open, closes 24 November 2026. HORIZON-TMA-MSCA-DN HORIZON TMA MSCA Doctoral Networks.

Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions Doctoral Networks are a principal mechanism for training the next generation of researchers across sectors and disciplines, with explicit attention to transferable skills and employability both inside and outside academia. The call emphasises inter-organisational cooperation, knowledge transfer between sectors, and internationalisation, making it especially relevant for consortia that want to embed doctoral candidates in genuinely cross-sector environments. The expected outcomes for participating organisations include boosted research and innovation capacity and improved sustainability of doctoral training programmes, which gives institutional partners a concrete rationale for joining.

Preparation step. Map the disciplinary and sectoral composition of your prospective consortium early, since the call explicitly rewards integration of training and research activities between participating organisations and enhanced cooperation across sectors; a clear matrix showing each partner's contribution to supervision, training modules, and knowledge transfer will strengthen the proposal from the outset.

Official topic page: HORIZON-MSCA-2026-DN-01-01

Structurally addressing homelessness through coordinated social infrastructure and services in neighbourhoods

Open, closes 1 December 2026. HORIZON-IA HORIZON Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: around EUR 3.5 million per grant, about 3 grants expected (2026).

This call responds directly to documented evidence that over 1.2 million people experience homelessness in Europe, with a large share in chronic situations lasting more than two years, and it frames homelessness as a structural and multidimensional problem requiring systemic neighbourhood-level intervention. By targeting social infrastructure and services rather than housing supply alone, it opens space for consortia that bring together urban planners, social service providers, and local authorities. The dual expected outcomes, generating evidence on what infrastructure is needed and actually improving availability and accessibility in targeted neighbourhoods, mean that both research rigour and practical implementation are evaluated.

Preparation step. Identify and document the specific neighbourhoods where your project will intervene, since the call requires evidence to be made available to stakeholders involved in tackling homelessness at neighbourhood level; grounding your proposal in concrete local contexts with named partners from social services and local government will directly address both expected outcomes.

Official topic page: HORIZON-NEB-2026-01-BUSINESS-01

EIC Accelerator 2026 - Short proposal

Open, closes 17 December 2026. HORIZON-EIC-ACC HORIZON EIC Accelerator.

The European Innovation Council Accelerator is specifically designed for companies, principally small and medium-sized enterprises and start-ups, that are developing deep-tech innovations built on scientific discovery or technological breakthroughs and require patient capital over a long timeframe. The call acknowledges that such innovations often cannot attract conventional financing because of the risks and time horizons involved, and it offers a combination of grant and investment funding alongside Business Acceleration Services to address that gap. For eligible companies, this represents a pathway to attract the full investment needed for scale-up in a shorter timeframe than would otherwise be possible.

Preparation step. Review Section VII of the European Innovation Council Work Programme 2026, which the call description explicitly references for Business Acceleration Services, and use that information to articulate in your short proposal how your company intends to engage with those services as part of its scale-up strategy.

Official topic page: HORIZON-EIC-2026-ACCELERATOR-01

Demonstrating place-based experimentation and innovation spaces as enabling environments for scaling EU Missions

Open, closes 13 January 2027. HORIZON-IA HORIZON Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: EUR 1.0 to 1.5 million per grant, about 3 grants expected (2026).

This call is positioned as a strategic instrument for the second phase of the European Union Missions lifecycle, explicitly focused on scaling up and wider deployment of novel mission-relevant solutions between 2026 and 2030. It calls on territories, including countries, regions, and interregional partnerships, to design and demonstrate governance and regulatory experimentation environments, which means that multi-level governance capacity is as important as technical innovation. The expected outcome of turning analytical insights into territorial best practices for replication across a wide array of European Union funding instruments gives successful projects an influence that extends well beyond the grant period.

Preparation step. Before drafting, define the specific European Union Mission or Missions your territory will anchor, then map the existing governance structures across the relevant public actors, because the call requires demonstration of multi-layered governance and public-public as well as public-private arrangements as enabling environments for scaling.

Official topic page: HORIZON-MISS-2026-07-CROSS-04

Dual-use technologies

Open, closes 14 January 2027. DIGITAL-JU-SIMPLE DIGITAL JU Simple Grants. Indicative budget: EUR 3.0 to 5.0 million per grant, about 3 grants expected (2026).

This call addresses the intersection of civilian and military cybersecurity by targeting standardised encrypted communication mechanisms, European-level early warning systems for cyber and hybrid threats, and integrated threat detection systems, all within a framework that must align with the General Data Protection Regulation and other European Union regulatory requirements. The inclusion of joint training programmes covering both civilian personnel, including law enforcement, and military personnel signals that the call values operational readiness alongside technical development. The scope explicitly covers predictive analytics for pre-emptive identification of threats to critical infrastructure, making it relevant for organisations with both technical and policy expertise.

Preparation step. Assemble a consortium that spans civilian and military or defence-adjacent stakeholders from the outset, since the call's expected outcomes, from the secure data sharing framework through to collaborative training programmes, each require coordinated input from both communities; document each partner's specific role in addressing the civilian-military interface before writing the technical sections.

Official topic page: DIGITAL-ECCC-2027-DEPLOY-CYBER-11-DUALUSE
