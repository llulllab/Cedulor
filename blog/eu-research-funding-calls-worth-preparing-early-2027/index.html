EU Research Funding Calls Worth Preparing For

By the Cedulor team. As of 25 September 2026.

The calls gathered here span a wide range of research and innovation priorities, from materials science and border security to doctoral training, bioenergy, and social infrastructure. Cedulor has prepared this briefing to help research offices and investigators assess fit, allocate preparation time wisely, and move into proposal development with a clear first step for each opportunity.

Advanced Materials for Miniaturised Energy Harvesting Systems

Open, closes 28 October 2026. HORIZON-EIC HORIZON EIC Grants. Indicative budget: EUR 0.5 to 4.0 million per grant, about 8 grants expected (2026).

This Pathfinder Challenge targets the discovery and development of advanced materials specifically for miniaturised energy harvesting, with a concrete ambition of reaching TRL 4 for energetically autonomous systems. The portfolio approach signals that the European Innovation Council expects a range of material types and application contexts, meaning proposals that carve out a distinct and credible niche are more likely to be selected. The alignment with RePowerEU and the Green Deal gives the call a strong sustainability framing that proposals must engage with substantively. Applications in point-of-care diagnostics and smart cities illustrate the breadth of downstream impact the call is designed to serve.

Preparation step. Map your proposed advanced material class against the portfolio logic described in the call: articulate clearly which gap in the landscape of miniaturised energy harvesting your approach fills, and prepare a concise TRL 4 roadmap showing how the project would demonstrate an energetically autonomous system by the end of the grant period.

Advanced border surveillance and situational awareness

Open, closes 5 November 2026. HORIZON-IA HORIZON Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: around EUR 6.0 million per grant, about 2 grants expected (2026).

Border surveillance and situational awareness at land and sea borders is the direct focus of this Innovation Action, with particular attention to detecting and characterising unmanned vehicles operating alone or in swarms. The call explicitly requires that solutions comply with legal and ethical guidelines and contribute to safeguarding fundamental rights, making those dimensions integral to the technical scope rather than optional additions. The emphasis on redundant detection and reaction capacities without putting staff or society at risk points to a need for robust, field-validated approaches. Large-scale population movements and the instrumentalisation of irregular migration are named as specific operational contexts that proposals must address.

Preparation step. Before drafting the technical approach, develop a structured ethical and legal compliance framework that maps each proposed surveillance or detection capability to the relevant rights and legal constraints named in the call, since this is presented as a core requirement rather than a secondary consideration.

MSCA Doctoral Networks 2026

Open, closes 24 November 2026. HORIZON-TMA-MSCA-DN-JD HORIZON TMA MSCA Doctoral Networks - Joint Doctorates.

This Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions Doctoral Networks call, specifically for Joint Doctorates, is designed to raise the quality and international reach of doctoral training through multi-sector, multi-disciplinary consortia. The expected outcomes for doctoral candidates include both research skills and transferable competences oriented toward careers inside and outside academia, reflecting the programme's longstanding commitment to researcher employability. For participating organisations, the call explicitly values enhanced cooperation across sectors and disciplines and increased integration of training with research activities. The internationalisation dimension is named as a priority outcome, reinforcing that the geographic and institutional spread of the consortium will be scrutinised.

Preparation step. Assemble the consortium with deliberate attention to sectoral and disciplinary diversity: because the call names cross-sector knowledge transfer and inter-organisational integration as distinct expected outcomes, each partner's specific contribution to doctoral training and supervision arrangements should be defined and documented well before the deadline.

Official topic page: HORIZON-MSCA-2026-DN-01-01

Demonstration of solid biofuel supply and conversion to high efficiency CHP from fully sustainable regional value chains

Open, closes 1 December 2026. HORIZON-IA HORIZON Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: around EUR 6.8 million per grant, about 2 grants expected (2026).

This Innovation Action calls for full demonstration of regional solid biofuel supply chains, from locally sourced upgraded biogenic residues and wastes through to high-efficiency combined heat and power conversion, in line with the Renewable Energy Directive. The regional value chain framing is central: the call explicitly links the demonstration to opportunities for small and medium-sized enterprises and to regional economic value generation, not merely to technical performance. Flexible renewable power and heat supply to local industries is named as a concrete outcome, grounding the call in real industrial demand. European competitiveness and energy security are stated motivations, situating the work within the broader policy context of the energy transition.

Preparation step. Identify and engage regional industrial heat and power offtakers early in the proposal development process, since the call's emphasis on demonstrating flexible supply to local industries means that credible demand-side partners are essential to establishing the regional value chain narrative the scope requires.

Official topic page: HORIZON-CL5-2026-11-D3-05

Structurally addressing homelessness through coordinated social infrastructure and services in neighbourhoods

Open, closes 1 December 2026. HORIZON-IA HORIZON Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: around EUR 3.5 million per grant, about 3 grants expected (2026).

This Innovation Action addresses homelessness as a structural and multidimensional challenge at neighbourhood level, explicitly moving beyond housing-led approaches to focus on the social infrastructure and services that must accompany them. The call references empirical data on the scale of homelessness in Europe and the prevalence of chronic homelessness, establishing an evidence base that proposals are expected to engage with and extend. Making evidence on structural social infrastructure needs available to stakeholders is itself a named expected outcome, meaning knowledge production and dissemination are as important as the interventions themselves. The neighbourhood scale is deliberate and definitional: proposals must demonstrate how systemic interventions can be designed and evaluated at that level.

Preparation step. Ground the proposal's intervention design in the specific neighbourhood contexts you intend to target, and plan from the outset how evidence on the social infrastructure and services tested will be packaged and made accessible to the wider stakeholder community named in the call, since that knowledge transfer is an explicit expected outcome.
