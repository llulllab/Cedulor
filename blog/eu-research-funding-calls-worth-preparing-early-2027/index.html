EU Research Funding Calls Worth Preparing For

By the Cedulor team. As of 26 September 2026.

Planning a competitive submission requires understanding what each call is actually asking for, not just its title. The calls below span materials science, disaster resilience, doctoral training, bioenergy, and social infrastructure, reflecting the breadth of research and innovation priorities across European funding programmes.

Advanced Materials for Miniaturised Energy Harvesting Systems

Open, closes 28 October 2026. HORIZON-EIC HORIZON EIC Grants. Indicative budget: EUR 0.5 to 4.0 million per grant, about 8 grants expected (2026).

This European Innovation Council Pathfinder Challenge targets the development of advanced materials specifically for miniaturised energy harvesting modules, with the concrete ambition of reaching Technology Readiness Level 4 for energetically autonomous systems. The call is directly tied to the goals of RePowerEU and the European Green Deal, giving successful projects strong policy relevance. Applications in point-of-care diagnostics and smart sustainable cities illustrate the breadth of societal impact expected. The portfolio approach means the European Innovation Council will seek complementary projects covering a variety of materials and system types, so a clearly differentiated materials focus strengthens any submission.

Preparation step. Map your proposed advanced materials against the full range of miniaturised energy harvesting module types described in the call, and articulate precisely how your approach will reach Technology Readiness Level 4 for the resulting energetically autonomous system, since that milestone is an explicit expected outcome.

Multi-hazard approach and cumulative / cascading impacts

Open, closes 5 November 2026. HORIZON-IA HORIZON Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: around EUR 4.0 million per grant, about 2 grants expected (2026).

This Innovation Action addresses one of the most complex challenges in disaster risk reduction: integrating single-hazard systems into multi-hazard predictive models that can capture cascading and cumulative effects across meteorological, geophysical, and technological hazards. The call explicitly seeks holistic risk and resilience metrics that encompass physical, economic, and social dimensions, making interdisciplinary consortia particularly well suited. Reliable, harmonised data collection at consistent granularity and format is identified as a core deliverable, pointing to the importance of data infrastructure in the proposal. The call also acknowledges the role of computational applications in supporting model development, which opens space for teams with strong modelling and simulation capabilities.

Preparation step. Develop a clear data management plan well before the deadline that specifies how your consortium will collect, harmonise, and format hazard and vulnerability data across the multiple hazard types named in the description, since data consistency at the same granularity is an explicit expected outcome of this call.

Official topic page: HORIZON-CL3-2026-01-DRS-02

MSCA Doctoral Networks 2026

Open, closes 24 November 2026. HORIZON-TMA-MSCA-DN-JD HORIZON TMA MSCA Doctoral Networks - Joint Doctorates.

The Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions Doctoral Networks Joint Doctorate scheme is designed to produce doctoral candidates with both deep research expertise and transferable skills that improve employability inside and outside academia. Participating organisations are expected to demonstrate improved cooperation and knowledge transfer between sectors and disciplines, making cross-sectoral consortium design a central consideration. The call emphasises internationalisation and boosted research and innovation capacity for all partners, not only the lead institution. For research offices, this means the quality and sustainability of the joint doctoral training programme itself will be scrutinised as carefully as the scientific content.

Preparation step. Draft a joint supervision and training programme framework that explicitly addresses how each participating organisation contributes to transferable skills development and cross-sector knowledge transfer, since the call evaluates the quality and sustainability of training arrangements as a primary expected outcome.

Official topic page: HORIZON-MSCA-2026-DN-01-01

Demonstration of solid biofuel supply and conversion to high efficiency CHP from fully sustainable regional value chains

Open, closes 1 December 2026. HORIZON-IA HORIZON Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: around EUR 6.8 million per grant, about 2 grants expected (2026).

This Innovation Action calls for full demonstration of regional value chains that source sustainable solid biofuel from upgraded biogenic residues and wastes and convert it in high-efficiency combined heat and power plants, in direct alignment with the Renewable Energy Directive. The call is explicitly oriented toward creating opportunities for small and medium-sized enterprises and generating regional economic value, which means proposals need to show concrete regional actors and supply chain structures. Flexible renewable power and heat delivery to local industries is highlighted as a key outcome, pointing to the importance of end-user engagement in the consortium. European competitiveness and international knowledge advancement in the bioenergy sector are stated ambitions, so proposals should articulate how demonstration results will be transferable beyond the pilot region.

Preparation step. Identify and engage regional biomass suppliers, waste upgrading facilities, and industrial heat and power end-users as consortium partners or stakeholders well before the deadline, because the call's expected outcomes centre on demonstrating a fully operational regional value chain rather than laboratory or pilot-scale results alone.

Official topic page: HORIZON-CL5-2026-11-D3-05

Structurally addressing homelessness through coordinated social infrastructure and services in neighbourhoods

Open, closes 1 December 2026. HORIZON-IA HORIZON Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: around EUR 3.5 million per grant, about 3 grants expected (2026).

This Innovation Action addresses homelessness as a structural and multidimensional challenge, focusing on the social infrastructure and services needed at neighbourhood level rather than on individual housing provision alone. The call draws on data showing that a significant proportion of homeless individuals experience chronic homelessness for over two years, underscoring the need for systemic interventions. Evidence generation for stakeholders involved in tackling homelessness is a primary expected outcome, so proposals must build in robust methods for producing and disseminating actionable findings. The neighbourhood scale of intervention is central to the call's logic, meaning that proposals should be grounded in specific, identifiable urban or peri-urban contexts.

Preparation step. Select and characterise the target neighbourhoods for your intervention well before submission, gathering existing data on local social infrastructure gaps and homeless population profiles, since the call requires evidence of the structural conditions at neighbourhood level to be made available to relevant stakeholders.
