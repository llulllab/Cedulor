EU Research Funding Calls Worth Preparing For

By the Cedulor team. As of 19 October 2026.

The calls below span doctoral training, bioenergy demonstration, social innovation, frontier research, and digital security, reflecting the breadth of European research and innovation funding. Reviewing each call's specific scope and expected outcomes well before the deadline will allow your team to align institutional strengths with what evaluators are actually looking for.

MSCA Doctoral Networks 2026

Open, closes 24 November 2026. HORIZON-TMA-MSCA-DN HORIZON TMA MSCA Doctoral Networks.

Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions Doctoral Networks are designed to raise the quality and international reach of doctoral training by connecting academic and non-academic organisations across sectors and disciplines. The call explicitly targets improved transferable skills, enhanced networking capacity, and boosted research and innovation capacity for both candidates and participating organisations. For institutions seeking to internationalise their doctoral programmes or build structured cross-sector training, this is a direct mechanism to do so. The emphasis on converting ideas into products and services also signals that industry co-design of training content is genuinely valued.

Preparation step. Map your institution's existing doctoral supervision arrangements against the call's stated outcomes on transferable skills and cross-sector knowledge transfer, identifying which external partners from industry or other disciplines could strengthen the training programme's relevance and sustainability.

Official topic page: HORIZON-MSCA-2026-DN-01-01

Demonstration of solid biofuel supply and conversion to high efficiency CHP from fully sustainable regional value chains

Open, closes 1 December 2026. HORIZON-IA HORIZON Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: around EUR 6.8 million per grant, about 2 grants expected (2026).

This call targets the full demonstration of regional value chains for solid biofuel supply and conversion in high-efficiency combined heat and power plants, with an explicit focus on locally sourced biogenic residues and wastes that comply with the Renewable Energy Directive. The scope connects renewable energy security with regional economic development and European competitiveness, making it relevant to consortia that can bridge agricultural or forestry waste streams with industrial heat and power demand. Demonstrating flexible renewable power and heat for local industrial processes is a central deliverable, not a secondary ambition.

Preparation step. Identify a regional cluster of biogenic residue and waste suppliers, a combined heat and power plant operator, and at least one local industrial heat consumer, then document the material flows and sustainability credentials of the feedstock against Renewable Energy Directive provisions before drafting the value chain narrative.

Official topic page: HORIZON-CL5-2026-11-D3-05

Structurally addressing homelessness through coordinated social infrastructure and services in neighbourhoods

Open, closes 1 December 2026. HORIZON-IA HORIZON Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: around EUR 3.5 million per grant, about 3 grants expected (2026).

The call responds to documented evidence that over 1.2 million people experience homelessness in Europe, with nearly half in chronic situations lasting more than two years, and it explicitly asks for systemic interventions at the neighbourhood level rather than individual housing placements alone. Projects must generate transferable evidence on the social infrastructure and services needed to address homelessness structurally, making rigorous documentation of neighbourhood-level conditions a core requirement. The framing around coordinated social infrastructure signals that multi-actor local partnerships involving municipalities, service providers, and community organisations are central to the approach.

Preparation step. Conduct a structured mapping of existing social infrastructure gaps and service coordination failures in your target neighbourhood, drawing on local administrative data and stakeholder input, so that the proposal can ground its intervention logic in concrete, place-specific evidence rather than general homelessness statistics.

Official topic page: HORIZON-NEB-2026-01-BUSINESS-01

ERC Consolidator Grant

Open, closes 12 January 2027. HORIZON-ERC HORIZON ERC Grants.

The European Research Council Consolidator Grant is designed for Principal Investigators who have already demonstrated research independence and are at the stage of consolidating their own team or programme. The call supports frontier research driven by the investigator's own vision, with a grant structure that allows for additional justified funding beyond the standard maximum when necessary to carry out the proposed work. This makes it one of the most flexible instruments available for ambitious individual research programmes at a critical career stage.

Preparation step. Prepare a clear account of your track record of research independence, including outputs and any team-building activities to date, since the call description identifies demonstrated independence as the foundational eligibility criterion for the Principal Investigator.

Official topic page: ERC-2027-COG

Strengthening cybersecurity capacities of European SMEs with cybersecure AI-powered solutions

Open, closes 14 January 2027. DIGITAL-JU-SME DIGITAL JU SME Support Actions. Indicative budget: EUR 3.0 to 5.0 million per grant, about 5 grants expected (2026).

This call under the Digital Europe programme focuses on deploying market-ready, computationally powered cybersecurity solutions to small and medium-sized enterprises, with explicit attention to integrating these tools into existing cybersecurity processes and training employees to use them effectively. It also encourages the uptake of results from previously funded Horizon Europe projects, creating a pathway for mature research outputs to reach the market through structured deployment activities. The dual emphasis on protecting SMEs and on securing the computational solutions themselves reflects a recognition that adoption without trustworthiness creates new vulnerabilities.

Preparation step. Compile a portfolio of market-ready computational cybersecurity tools that are either already validated in prior European research projects or commercially available, and define a concrete deployment and training methodology tailored to the operational constraints and skill levels typical of SME environments.

Official topic page: DIGITAL-ECCC-2027-DEPLOY-CYBER-11-AI4SME
