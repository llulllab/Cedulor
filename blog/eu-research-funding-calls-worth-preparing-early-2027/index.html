EU Research Funding Calls Worth Preparing For

By the Cedulor team. As of 29 September 2026.

The calls below span a wide range of research and innovation priorities, from advanced materials and border security to doctoral training, bioenergy, and social infrastructure. Cedulor has surfaced the key preparation angle for each so that your team can focus effort where it counts most well before the deadline.

Advanced Materials for Miniaturised Energy Harvesting Systems

Open, closes 28 October 2026. HORIZON-EIC HORIZON EIC Grants. Indicative budget: EUR 0.5 to 4.0 million per grant, about 8 grants expected (2026).

This Pathfinder Challenge targets a genuinely foundational question: what new classes of advanced materials can make miniaturised energy harvesting viable at scale. The expected outcome sets a clear technical milestone, TRL 4 for energetically autonomous systems, which means proposals must demonstrate real proof-of-concept ambition rather than conceptual work alone. The call is explicitly framed around a portfolio approach, meaning the selection panel will be looking for proposals that contribute distinct material directions rather than converging on the same solution. Alignment with RePowerEU and the Green Deal gives the work strong policy grounding that reviewers will expect to see reflected in the impact narrative.

Preparation step. Map your proposed advanced material against the portfolio logic described in the call: articulate clearly which underexplored material family or harvesting mechanism your project addresses, and explain how reaching TRL 4 for the resulting autonomous system is achievable within the project scope, since reviewers will assess whether the ambition is credible and the trajectory toward energetic autonomy is technically coherent.

Advanced border surveillance and situational awareness

Open, closes 5 November 2026. HORIZON-IA HORIZON Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: around EUR 6.0 million per grant, about 2 grants expected (2026).

Border surveillance at land and sea frontiers is increasingly complex, and this Innovation Action addresses both the technical and the normative dimensions: detection and neutralisation of unmanned vehicles must be balanced against legal and ethical compliance. The call also foregrounds the challenge of large-scale population movements, including scenarios involving the instrumentalisation of irregular migration, which broadens the operational context well beyond conventional surveillance. Redundancy and staff safety are explicitly listed as expected outcomes, signalling that resilience of the proposed systems will be scrutinised. For research offices, the dual-use sensitivity of this topic means that consortium composition and ethical safeguards will need careful thought from the outset.

Preparation step. Before drafting the technical approach, prepare a dedicated ethical and legal compliance framework that directly addresses the guidelines referenced in the call description, covering how your detection and neutralisation capabilities will operate within fundamental rights obligations and how the proposal will document those safeguards for reviewers.

Official topic page: HORIZON-CL3-2026-01-BM-01

MSCA Doctoral Networks 2026

Open, closes 24 November 2026. HORIZON-TMA-MSCA-DN-JD HORIZON TMA MSCA Doctoral Networks - Joint Doctorates.

Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions Doctoral Networks remain one of the most structured mechanisms in European research funding for building long-term training capacity across sectors and disciplines. The Joint Doctorate strand in particular demands a high degree of integration between participating organisations, not just in supervision but in the actual design of training and research activities. The call description emphasises transferable skills, employability both inside and outside academia, and the conversion of ideas into products and services, which means purely academic consortia will need to demonstrate genuine non-academic engagement. Internationalisation is also flagged as a key outcome, so the geographic and sectoral spread of partners carries real evaluative weight.

Preparation step. Develop a joint supervision and training matrix well before the deadline: for each doctoral candidate position, specify which participating organisations share supervisory responsibility, which transferable skill modules are delivered by non-academic partners, and how the programme design ensures the knowledge-transfer outcomes the call description explicitly requires.

Official topic page: HORIZON-MSCA-2026-DN-01-01

Demonstration of solid biofuel supply and conversion to high efficiency CHP from fully sustainable regional value chains

Open, closes 1 December 2026. HORIZON-IA HORIZON Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: around EUR 6.8 million per grant, about 2 grants expected (2026).

This Innovation Action focuses on demonstration at a regional scale, meaning the call is not asking for further research into biofuel conversion in the abstract but for evidence that full value chains, from locally sourced biogenic residues and wastes through to high-efficiency combined heat and power, can work in practice. Compliance with the Renewable Energy Directive is a stated condition, not a background assumption, so proposals will need to show that the feedstocks and processes meet those provisions. The call also highlights opportunities for small and medium-sized enterprises and regional value generation, which shapes the kind of consortium and dissemination strategy that will be credible. Flexible renewable power and heat for local industrial processes is an explicit outcome, pointing toward the need for real industrial end-users in the project.

Preparation step. Identify and engage regional industrial end-users and feedstock suppliers as consortium members or named stakeholders well before submission, since the demonstration logic of the call requires evidence that locally sourced sustainable biogenic residues are genuinely available in the target region and that industrial heat and power offtake is already committed or plausibly committed.

Official topic page: HORIZON-CL5-2026-11-D3-05

Structurally addressing homelessness through coordinated social infrastructure and services in neighbourhoods

Open, closes 1 December 2026. HORIZON-IA HORIZON Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: around EUR 3.5 million per grant, about 3 grants expected (2026).

This call sits within the New European Bauhaus context and addresses homelessness as a structural, neighbourhood-level challenge rather than an individual one, a framing that has direct implications for how proposals must be designed. The description cites data showing that a large share of homeless individuals experience chronic homelessness lasting over two years, which sets the evaluative bar: interventions must be systemic and evidence-generating, not short-term service pilots. Both the availability and the accessibility of social infrastructure and services are listed as expected outcomes, so proposals need to address physical and organisational barriers simultaneously. The call explicitly calls for evidence to be made available to stakeholders, meaning a knowledge-transfer and dissemination plan oriented toward practitioners and policymakers is not optional.

Preparation step. Ground the proposal in a clearly defined neighbourhood or set of neighbourhoods where the structural gaps in social infrastructure and services for homeless groups can be documented with existing data, since the call requires that evidence of what is needed be made available to stakeholders, and reviewers will expect the project to start from a well-characterised local reality rather than a generic intervention model.

Official topic page: HORIZON-NEB-2026-01-BUSINESS-01
