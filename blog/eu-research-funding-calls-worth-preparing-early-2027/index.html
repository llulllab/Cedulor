EU Research Funding Calls Worth Preparing For

By the Cedulor team. As of 25 September 2026.

The calls below span a wide range of research and innovation priorities, from advanced materials and border security to doctoral training, bioenergy, and social infrastructure. Each represents a distinct funding opportunity with its own strategic logic, eligibility conditions, and preparation demands. Reviewing them carefully well before any deadline will help your team allocate effort where it counts most.

Advanced Materials for Miniaturised Energy Harvesting Systems

Open, closes 28 October 2026. HORIZON-EIC HORIZON EIC Grants. Indicative budget: EUR 0.5 to 4.0 million per grant, about 8 grants expected (2026).

This Pathfinder Challenge targets the development of a new generation of advanced materials specifically for miniaturised energy harvesting modules, with the explicit goal of reaching Technology Readiness Level 4 for energetically autonomous systems. The call is directly tied to the ambitions of RePowerEU and the Green Deal, meaning proposals must credibly connect materials science to sustainability objectives. Applications in point-of-care diagnostics and smart sustainable cities are highlighted as illustrative impact areas, giving consortia concrete domains in which to frame their work. The portfolio approach signals that the European Innovation Council expects a spread of material types and application contexts across selected projects.

Preparation step. Map your proposed advanced materials against the TRL 4 milestone explicitly required by the call, and document the pathway from current readiness to that target so reviewers can assess the ambition and feasibility of your plan from the outset.

Advanced border surveillance and situational awareness

Open, closes 5 November 2026. HORIZON-IA HORIZON Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: around EUR 6.0 million per grant, about 2 grants expected (2026).

This Innovation Action addresses a pressing operational need: improving detection, localisation, characterisation, and where appropriate neutralisation of unmanned vehicles involved in cross-border trafficking and smuggling. The scope also encompasses large-scale movements of people, including scenarios involving the instrumentalisation of irregular migration, and explicitly requires that solutions comply with legal and ethical guidelines while safeguarding fundamental rights. The call stresses reliability and redundancy so that detection and reaction capacities do not put staff or society at risk. These dual requirements, technical robustness and rights compliance, make the call distinctive and demanding.

Preparation step. Prepare a dedicated section of your proposal that maps each technical capability you plan to develop directly onto the legal and ethical compliance requirements stated in the call, showing how your approach protects fundamental rights while delivering the required detection and situational awareness outcomes.

Official topic page: HORIZON-CL3-2026-01-BM-01

MSCA Doctoral Networks 2026

Open, closes 24 November 2026. HORIZON-TMA-MSCA-DN-JD HORIZON TMA MSCA Doctoral Networks - Joint Doctorates.

Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions Doctoral Networks under the Joint Doctorate strand fund structured, multi-institutional doctoral training programmes designed to produce researchers with both deep disciplinary knowledge and broad transferable skills. The call explicitly values enhanced cooperation and knowledge transfer between sectors and disciplines, increased internationalisation, and the integration of training and research activities across participating organisations. For supported doctoral candidates, the expected outcomes include improved employability within and outside academia and the capacity to convert ideas into products and services. This makes the call relevant not only to universities but also to non-academic partners seeking to build longer-term research relationships.

Preparation step. Identify and formally engage non-academic partners early in the design process, since the call description emphasises cross-sector knowledge transfer and integration of training activities between participating organisations as core expected outcomes for the consortium as a whole.

Official topic page: HORIZON-MSCA-2026-DN-01-01

Demonstration of solid biofuel supply and conversion to high efficiency CHP from fully sustainable regional value chains

Open, closes 1 December 2026. HORIZON-IA HORIZON Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: around EUR 6.8 million per grant, about 2 grants expected (2026).

This Innovation Action calls for the demonstration of complete regional value chains, from locally or regionally sourced biogenic residues and wastes through to high-efficiency combined heat and power generation, all in line with the Renewable Energy Directive. The call explicitly links success to European competitiveness, energy security, and climate targets, and highlights opportunities for small and medium-sized enterprises and regional value generation. Providing flexible renewable power and heat to local industries is framed as a concrete deliverable, not merely a secondary benefit. The emphasis on fully sustainable and cheap solid biofuel supply means the sustainability credentials of the feedstock must be rigorously demonstrated.

Preparation step. Develop a detailed regional feedstock mapping exercise well before the deadline to confirm that your supply chain uses locally or regionally sourced biogenic residues and wastes that meet the Renewable Energy Directive provisions, since the sustainability of the entire value chain is a central evaluation criterion.

Official topic page: HORIZON-CL5-2026-11-D3-05

Structurally addressing homelessness through coordinated social infrastructure and services in neighbourhoods

Open, closes 1 December 2026. HORIZON-IA HORIZON Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: around EUR 3.5 million per grant, about 3 grants expected (2026).

This Innovation Action responds to evidence that over 1.2 million people experience homelessness in Europe, with nearly half in chronic situations lasting more than two years, and calls for systemic interventions at neighbourhood level rather than individual housing solutions alone. The call explicitly requires that projects generate evidence on the structural social infrastructure and services needed to address homelessness and improve their availability and accessibility for homeless groups in targeted neighbourhoods. The framing around coordinated social infrastructure signals that proposals must go beyond single-service interventions and address the multidimensional causes of homelessness. Stakeholders involved in tackling homelessness are identified as the primary audience for the evidence produced.

Preparation step. Define your target neighbourhoods and the specific stakeholder groups who will use the evidence your project generates, since the call requires that findings on structural social infrastructure and services be made available to those stakeholders and that accessibility for homeless groups in those locations demonstrably improves.
