EU Research Funding Calls Worth Preparing For

By the Cedulor team. As of 17 October 2026.

The calls below span doctoral training, bioenergy demonstration, social innovation, frontier research, and territorial governance, reflecting the breadth of what European research and innovation funding can support. Reviewing each call's scope and expected outcomes carefully will help your team identify where your organisation's strengths align most naturally.

MSCA Doctoral Networks 2026

Open, closes 24 November 2026. HORIZON-TMA-MSCA-DN HORIZON TMA MSCA Doctoral Networks.

Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions Doctoral Networks are designed to raise the quality and international reach of doctoral training by bringing together organisations across sectors and disciplines. The call explicitly targets improvements in transferable skills, employability inside and outside academia, and the conversion of research ideas into products and services. For participating organisations, the expected outcomes include boosted research and innovation capacity and deeper integration of training with research activities. This makes the call particularly relevant for universities, research institutes, and companies that want to build or strengthen cross-sectoral doctoral programmes.

Preparation step. Map your organisation's existing doctoral supervision arrangements and identify potential non-academic partners whose involvement would directly address the call's emphasis on cross-sector knowledge transfer and enhanced cooperation between disciplines.

Demonstration of solid biofuel supply and conversion to high efficiency CHP from fully sustainable regional value chains

Open, closes 1 December 2026. HORIZON-IA HORIZON Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: around EUR 6.8 million per grant, about 2 grants expected (2026).

This call targets the real-world demonstration of regional value chains that use locally sourced sustainable solid biofuels, converted through high-efficiency combined heat and power plants, to contribute to EU climate and energy targets. The description stresses alignment with the Renewable Energy Directive and the creation of opportunities for small and medium-sized enterprises through regional value generation. Flexible renewable power and heat supply to local industries is a central expected outcome, making this call directly relevant to regions with biogenic residue streams that are currently underutilised. European knowledge advancement and international competitiveness in the bioenergy sector are explicitly named as goals.

Preparation step. Conduct a regional audit of available biogenic residues and waste streams to confirm that your proposed value chain can credibly demonstrate the locally to regionally sourced supply model the description requires, and document how it aligns with Renewable Energy Directive provisions.

Official topic page: HORIZON-CL5-2026-11-D3-05

Structurally addressing homelessness through coordinated social infrastructure and services in neighbourhoods

Open, closes 1 December 2026. HORIZON-IA HORIZON Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: around EUR 3.5 million per grant, about 3 grants expected (2026).

The call responds directly to evidence that over 1.2 million people experience homelessness in Europe, with nearly half in chronic situations lasting more than two years, and it calls for systemic interventions at neighbourhood level rather than individual housing solutions alone. Projects are expected to generate evidence on the structural social infrastructure and services needed, and to improve their availability and accessibility for homeless groups in targeted neighbourhoods. This framing means the call is suited to consortia that can combine urban governance, social services, and community-level implementation. The neighbourhood scale is deliberate and central to the expected outcomes.

Preparation step. Define the specific neighbourhoods where your project would intervene and gather baseline data on the social infrastructure and services currently available there, since the call's expected outcomes require demonstrable improvements in availability and accessibility at that precise geographic level.

Official topic page: HORIZON-NEB-2026-01-BUSINESS-01

ERC Consolidator Grant

Open, closes 12 January 2027. HORIZON-ERC HORIZON ERC Grants.

The European Research Council Consolidator Grant is designed for principal investigators who have already demonstrated research independence and are consolidating their own team or programme, making the track record of the individual the central evaluative criterion. The call supports investigator-driven frontier research without prescribing a thematic area, which places the entire scientific case on the originality and ambition of the proposed work. The grant structure allows for additional funding beyond the standard ceiling when justified by the nature of the work, so proposals with genuinely demanding methodological or infrastructural requirements have a route to request it. Because the grant runs for up to sixty months, reviewers will scrutinise whether the scientific vision is coherent and sufficiently ambitious to sustain a full research programme over that period.

Preparation step. Prepare a rigorous and honest audit of the principal investigator's publication record, independent research outputs, and evidence of team leadership, since the call's own framing makes demonstrated independence the threshold condition; this audit should inform how the scientific narrative is constructed rather than being treated as a separate administrative task.

Official topic page: ERC-2027-COG

Demonstrating place-based experimentation and innovation spaces as enabling environments for scaling EU Missions

Open, closes 13 January 2027. HORIZON-IA HORIZON Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: EUR 1.0 to 1.5 million per grant, about 3 grants expected (2026).

This call is explicitly positioned as a strategic instrument for the second phase of the European Union Missions lifecycle, focused on scaling up novel solutions rather than initial piloting. It calls on territories, including countries, regions, and interregional partnerships, to design and demonstrate governance and regulatory experimentation environments that can be replicated across a wide array of European Union funding instruments. The expected outcomes include strengthened territorial anchoring of Missions through multi-layered governance and the translation of analytical insights into best practices. Consortia that can bridge territorial authorities, research actors, and innovation intermediaries are well placed to respond.

Preparation step. Identify which European Union Mission or Missions your territory's existing strategies already address, and prepare documentation showing how your governance structures could support regulatory experimentation and public-public or public-private collaboration at the scale the description envisions.
