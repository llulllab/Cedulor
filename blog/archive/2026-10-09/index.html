EU Research Funding Calls Worth Preparing For

By the Cedulor team. As of 9 October 2026.

This is the edition of 9 October 2026. The dates and statuses below are as they stood on that day and may have changed since. Read the current edition.

The calls below span doctoral training, social innovation, deep tech scale-up, territorial experimentation, and dual-use cybersecurity, reflecting the breadth of European research and innovation funding. Reviewing each call's scope and expected outcomes well before the deadline will help your team allocate preparation time wisely.

MSCA Doctoral Networks 2026

Open, closes 24 November 2026. HORIZON-TMA-MSCA-DN HORIZON TMA MSCA Doctoral Networks.

Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions Doctoral Networks are designed to raise the quality and international reach of doctoral training by connecting universities, research institutes, and non-academic partners across sectors and disciplines. The call explicitly targets both the candidates themselves, building transferable skills and employability, and the participating organisations, strengthening their research and innovation capacity. The emphasis on knowledge transfer between sectors and on converting research ideas into products and services makes this particularly relevant for consortia that span academia and industry. Internationalisation is a stated goal, so cross-border partnerships are central to a competitive proposal.

Preparation step. Map your prospective consortium against the call's dual focus: identify which participating organisations will contribute to inter-sectoral knowledge transfer and which supervisory arrangements will demonstrably improve doctoral training quality, since both dimensions are explicit expected outcomes.

Structurally addressing homelessness through coordinated social infrastructure and services in neighbourhoods

Open, closes 1 December 2026. HORIZON-IA HORIZON Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: around EUR 3.5 million per grant, about 3 grants expected (2026).

This call addresses one of Europe's most persistent social challenges: the description cites over 1.2 million people experiencing homelessness across Europe, with nearly half in chronic situations lasting more than two years. The scope goes beyond housing-led approaches to demand systemic interventions in social infrastructure and services at the neighbourhood level, making it relevant for consortia that combine urban planning, social services, and local governance expertise. Evidence generation for stakeholders is an explicit outcome, so the call rewards rigorous documentation of what structural conditions actually reduce homelessness.

Preparation step. Define the specific neighbourhoods and the mix of social infrastructure and services your project will target, since the call requires both evidence of what is structurally needed and demonstrated improvements in availability and accessibility for homeless groups in those locations.

EIC Accelerator 2026 - Short proposal

Open, closes 17 December 2026. HORIZON-EIC-ACC HORIZON EIC Accelerator.

The European Innovation Council Accelerator is designed for companies, principally small and medium-sized enterprises and start-ups, that are developing deep tech innovations requiring significant funding over a long timeframe before returns can be generated. The call explicitly acknowledges that such innovations struggle to attract conventional financing because of the risks and time horizons involved, and it responds with a combination of grant and investment funding alongside Business Acceleration Services. The focus on scientific discovery or technological breakthroughs means that a strong link between the innovation and an underlying research base is expected.

Preparation step. Prepare a clear articulation of the scientific discovery or technological breakthrough underpinning your innovation and explain why the scale-up requires patient capital, since the call's rationale rests precisely on that combination of deep tech origin and financing gap.

Official topic page: HORIZON-EIC-2026-ACCELERATOR-01

Demonstrating place-based experimentation and innovation spaces as enabling environments for scaling EU Missions

Open, closes 13 January 2027. HORIZON-IA HORIZON Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: EUR 1.0 to 1.5 million per grant, about 3 grants expected (2026).

This call serves as a strategic instrument for the second phase of the European Union Missions lifecycle, explicitly covering the period focused on scaling up and wider deployment of mission-relevant solutions. It calls on territories, including countries, regions, and interregional partnerships, to design and demonstrate governance and regulatory experimentation environments, which positions it as an opportunity for public authorities and regional bodies with a mandate to pilot novel approaches. The expected outcomes include turning analytical insights into territorial best practices that can be replicated across a wide array of European Union funding instruments, amplifying the reach of any successful project.

Preparation step. Engage regional or national authorities as core partners early, because the call specifically addresses territorial anchoring and multi-layered governance as expected outcomes, and those partners will need to demonstrate a mandate for regulatory experimentation within their jurisdiction.

Official topic page: HORIZON-MISS-2026-07-CROSS-04

Dual-use technologies

Open, closes 14 January 2027. DIGITAL-JU-SIMPLE DIGITAL JU Simple Grants. Indicative budget: EUR 3.0 to 5.0 million per grant, about 3 grants expected (2026).

This call targets the development of dual-use technologies that serve both civilian and military stakeholders, with a scope covering encrypted data sharing frameworks, European-level early warning systems for cyber and hybrid threats, and integrated threat detection systems. The explicit requirement to align with GDPR and other European Union regulatory requirements means that legal and compliance expertise is as important as technical capability. The inclusion of joint training programmes for civilian, law enforcement, and military personnel broadens the type of organisations that can contribute meaningfully to a proposal.

Preparation step. Assemble a consortium that covers the civilian-military boundary from the outset, since the call's expected outcomes span GDPR-compliant data sharing, real-time monitoring for critical infrastructure, and sector-specific training scenarios, each of which requires distinct organisational competences working in coordination.

Official topic page: DIGITAL-ECCC-2027-DEPLOY-CYBER-11-DUALUSE
