EU Research Funding Calls Worth Preparing For

By the Cedulor team. As of 25 October 2026.

This is the edition of 25 October 2026. The dates and statuses below are as they stood on that day and may have changed since. Read the current edition.

The calls below span a wide arc of European research and innovation priorities, from doctoral training and frontier science to bioenergy demonstration, social infrastructure, and territorial governance. Planning well before each deadline means understanding not just the funding rules but the specific outcomes each call is designed to produce. Cedulor maps those outcomes to your project concept so you can build a stronger, more targeted proposal.

MSCA Doctoral Networks 2026

Open, closes 24 November 2026. HORIZON-TMA-MSCA-DN HORIZON TMA MSCA Doctoral Networks.

Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions Doctoral Networks are one of the primary European instruments for building research capacity across sectors and disciplines, and this call explicitly targets improvements in doctoral training quality, transferable skills, and the conversion of research ideas into products and services. The emphasis on cross-sector knowledge transfer and internationalisation means that consortia bringing together academic and non-academic partners from multiple countries are strongly positioned. For research offices, this call also offers an opportunity to deepen institutional cooperation and boost the organisation's own research and innovation capacity as a participating host.

Preparation step. Map your institution's existing doctoral supervision arrangements against the call's stated outcomes around training quality, transferable skills, and cross-sector knowledge transfer, then identify non-academic partners whose involvement would directly address the gaps the call expects projects to close.

Official topic page: HORIZON-MSCA-2026-DN-01-01

Demonstration of solid biofuel supply and conversion to high efficiency CHP from fully sustainable regional value chains

Open, closes 1 December 2026. HORIZON-IA HORIZON Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: around EUR 6.8 million per grant, about 2 grants expected (2026).

This call targets the full regional value chain for solid biofuel, from sustainably sourced biogenic residues and wastes through to high-efficiency combined heat and power conversion, which means proposals must demonstrate real-world integration across supply, upgrading, and conversion stages. The description ties the expected outcomes explicitly to the Renewable Energy Directive, European energy security, and competitiveness, giving projects a clear regulatory and policy anchor. Opportunities for small and medium-sized enterprises and for regional value generation are highlighted, making this particularly relevant for consortia that can show local economic impact alongside technical performance.

Preparation step. Audit the regional availability of biogenic residues and wastes that qualify under the Renewable Energy Directive provisions, and use that evidence as the foundation for scoping a value chain that connects local supply actors with a combined heat and power demonstration site.

Official topic page: HORIZON-CL5-2026-11-D3-05

Structurally addressing homelessness through coordinated social infrastructure and services in neighbourhoods

Open, closes 1 December 2026. HORIZON-IA HORIZON Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: around EUR 3.5 million per grant, about 3 grants expected (2026).

The call is grounded in documented evidence that over 1.2 million people experience homelessness in Europe, with nearly half in chronic situations lasting more than two years, which gives proposals a concrete empirical baseline to build from. It specifically calls for systemic, structural interventions at the neighbourhood level, meaning that projects focused solely on individual housing provision will not fully meet the scope. The dual focus on generating evidence for stakeholders and on improving the actual availability of social infrastructure and services in targeted neighbourhoods requires a design that combines rigorous data collection with tangible on-the-ground implementation.

Preparation step. Select one or more target neighbourhoods where your consortium has existing relationships with local authorities and social service providers, then define the specific structural gaps in social infrastructure you will both document and address, so that the proposal directly mirrors the call's two-part evidence-and-improvement logic.

Official topic page: HORIZON-NEB-2026-01-BUSINESS-01

ERC Consolidator Grant

Open, closes 12 January 2027. HORIZON-ERC HORIZON ERC Grants.

The European Research Council Consolidator Grant is directed at principal investigators who have already demonstrated research independence and are at the stage of consolidating their own team or programme. The grant supports frontier research defined entirely by scientific excellence and the investigator's own vision, with no thematic restriction imposed by the funder. The description specifies a maximum grant duration of sixty months and allows for an additional funding request for eligible costs when duly justified in the proposal. That justification requirement means the budget narrative must be substantive and directly tied to the proposed work.

Preparation step. Draft a clear account of how your research programme demonstrates independence from your doctoral and postdoctoral supervisors, since the call explicitly requires evidence of research independence as a condition of eligibility for the principal investigator.

Official topic page: ERC-2027-COG

Demonstrating place-based experimentation and innovation spaces as enabling environments for scaling EU Missions

Open, closes 13 January 2027. HORIZON-IA HORIZON Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: EUR 1.0 to 1.5 million per grant, about 3 grants expected (2026).

This call is explicitly positioned as a strategic instrument for the second phase of the European Union Missions lifecycle, oriented toward scaling and wider deployment rather than initial exploration, which means proposals need to show readiness to move from analytical insight to territorial experimentation. The scope calls on countries, regions, and interregional partnerships to design and demonstrate governance and regulatory experimentation, including public-public and public-private arrangements, so the institutional and political dimension of the consortium is as important as the technical content. The expectation that best practices will be replicable across a wide array of European Union funding instruments means proposals should be designed with transferability built in from the start.

Preparation step. Identify the specific European Union Mission or Missions your territory intends to anchor, then document the existing governance structures and any regulatory sandboxes or experimentation mechanisms already in place, so the proposal can demonstrate a credible starting point for the scaling logic the call requires.
