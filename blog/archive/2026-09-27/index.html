EU Research Funding Calls Worth Preparing For

By the Cedulor team. As of 27 September 2026.

This is the edition of 27 September 2026. The dates and statuses below are as they stood on that day and may have changed since. Read the current edition.

The calls below span a wide range of research and innovation priorities, from materials science and border security to doctoral training, bioenergy, and social infrastructure. Reviewing each call's scope and expected outcomes carefully will help your team decide where your institution's strengths align best and how to frame a compelling proposal.

Advanced Materials for Miniaturised Energy Harvesting Systems

Open, closes 28 October 2026. HORIZON-EIC HORIZON EIC Grants. Indicative budget: EUR 0.5 to 4.0 million per grant, about 8 grants expected (2026).

This Pathfinder Challenge targets the development of advanced materials specifically for miniaturised energy harvesting modules, with a concrete technical milestone of reaching TRL 4 for energetically autonomous systems. The expected impact connects directly to sustainability goals embedded in RePowerEU and the Green Deal, as well as to real-world applications such as point-of-care diagnostics and smart cities. Because the European Innovation Council will build a portfolio of selected projects covering a variety of materials and applications, proposals that clearly define a distinct materials approach and application domain stand a stronger chance of complementing rather than duplicating others in that portfolio.

Preparation step. Map your proposed advanced materials against the full range of miniaturised energy harvesting applications mentioned in the description, such as point-of-care diagnostics and smart sustainable cities, and articulate clearly which application domain your consortium addresses and why your materials pathway is technically credible for reaching TRL 4.

Advanced border surveillance and situational awareness

Open, closes 5 November 2026. HORIZON-IA HORIZON Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: around EUR 6.0 million per grant, about 2 grants expected (2026).

This Innovation Action addresses concrete operational gaps in land and sea border surveillance, including the detection and neutralisation of unmanned vehicles in swarms and the management of large-scale population movements. The scope explicitly requires that solutions comply with legal and ethical guidelines and safeguard fundamental rights, making the integration of those considerations a substantive technical requirement rather than an afterthought. Reliable, redundant detection and reaction capacities that do not put staff or society at risk are a named outcome, signalling that system resilience and safety must be built into the proposal's core design.

Preparation step. Before drafting the technical approach, prepare a structured analysis of how your proposed surveillance solution addresses the legal and ethical compliance requirements stated in the description, since the call explicitly names this as a condition for improved detection and neutralisation capabilities.

Official topic page: HORIZON-CL3-2026-01-BM-01

MSCA Doctoral Networks 2026

Open, closes 24 November 2026. HORIZON-TMA-MSCA-DN-JD HORIZON TMA MSCA Doctoral Networks - Joint Doctorates.

Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions Doctoral Networks under the Joint Doctorate track require coordinated training programmes across multiple participating organisations, with an explicit emphasis on transferable skills, cross-sector knowledge transfer, and internationalisation. The expected outcomes for participating organisations include improved sustainability of doctoral training and boosted research and innovation capacity, meaning the institutional design of the network is evaluated alongside the scientific content. The focus on converting ideas into products and services where relevant also signals that industry or non-academic partners can strengthen the proposal's profile.

Preparation step. Convene your prospective partner organisations well before the deadline to agree on a shared supervision framework and to document how each partner's distinct sectoral or disciplinary profile contributes to the cross-sector knowledge transfer and internationalisation outcomes the call describes.

Official topic page: HORIZON-MSCA-2026-DN-01-01

Demonstration of solid biofuel supply and conversion to high efficiency CHP from fully sustainable regional value chains

Open, closes 1 December 2026. HORIZON-IA HORIZON Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: around EUR 6.8 million per grant, about 2 grants expected (2026).

This Innovation Action calls for the demonstration of complete regional value chains, from locally sourced sustainable solid biofuel through to high-efficiency combined heat and power conversion, with alignment to the Renewable Energy Directive. The description highlights opportunities for small and medium-sized enterprises and for regional value generation, meaning the proposal should reflect genuine regional embeddedness rather than a generic technology demonstration. Flexible renewable power and heat supply to local industries is a named outcome, so the industrial demand side of the value chain needs to be addressed explicitly.

Preparation step. Identify and engage regional biomass suppliers, waste and residue holders, and local industrial heat consumers early in proposal development, so that the value chain you describe is grounded in verifiable regional supply and demand relationships consistent with the Renewable Energy Directive provisions the call references.

Official topic page: HORIZON-CL5-2026-11-D3-05

Structurally addressing homelessness through coordinated social infrastructure and services in neighbourhoods

Open, closes 1 December 2026. HORIZON-IA HORIZON Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: around EUR 3.5 million per grant, about 3 grants expected (2026).

This Innovation Action responds to documented evidence that over 1.2 million people experience homelessness in Europe, with a substantial share in chronic situations lasting more than two years, and it focuses specifically on the neighbourhood level as the unit of intervention. The call distinguishes between housing-led approaches and the structural, multidimensional causes of homelessness, asking for systemic interventions that improve the availability and accessibility of social infrastructure and services. Generating evidence that can be made available to stakeholders tackling homelessness is an explicit outcome, so the proposal must include a credible knowledge-transfer and dissemination component directed at practitioners and policymakers.

Preparation step. Define the specific neighbourhoods and homeless groups your project will target, and build a stakeholder engagement plan that connects the evidence you will generate to the practitioners and decision-makers who can act on it, since making that evidence available to relevant stakeholders is stated as a required outcome.
