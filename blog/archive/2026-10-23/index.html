EU Research Funding Calls Worth Preparing For

By the Cedulor team. As of 23 October 2026.

This is the edition of 23 October 2026. The dates and statuses below are as they stood on that day and may have changed since. Read the current edition.

The calls below span a wide arc of research and innovation priorities, from deep geothermal energy and environmental contamination to urban social infrastructure, territorial governance, and cybersecurity for smaller enterprises. Reviewing each call's scope and expected outcomes carefully will help research offices identify the best strategic fit and begin assembling the right consortium or organisational profile well before the deadline.

Resource assessment for deep sedimentary and basement reservoirs

Open, closes 1 December 2026. HORIZON-RIA HORIZON Research and Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: around EUR 4.5 million per grant, about 4 grants expected (2026).

Deep sedimentary and basement reservoirs remain largely unexplored for geothermal purposes because of low permeability, high drilling costs, and scarce data. This call directly targets the knowledge gaps that prevent developers from identifying suitable locations for approaches such as Enhanced Geothermal Systems and closed-loop geothermal systems. Funding research here can increase drilling success rates, improve geothermal performance, and reduce the risk of induced seismicity, all of which are explicitly named as expected outcomes.

Preparation step. Map the existing data scarcity in your target reservoir type and build your proposal around a clear methodology for predicting reservoir structures and properties, since the description places predicting deep sedimentary and basement reservoir characteristics at the centre of the scientific challenge.

Official topic page: HORIZON-CL5-2026-11-D3-06

Structurally addressing homelessness through coordinated social infrastructure and services in neighbourhoods

Open, closes 1 December 2026. HORIZON-IA HORIZON Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: around EUR 3.5 million per grant, about 3 grants expected (2026).

With over 1.2 million people estimated to experience homelessness in Europe and nearly half of surveyed individuals in chronic homelessness for more than two years, the scale of the problem demands systemic, neighbourhood-level responses. This call goes beyond housing-led approaches to address the structural and multidimensional causes of homelessness through coordinated social infrastructure and services. Projects are expected to produce concrete evidence of what infrastructure and services are needed and to improve their availability and accessibility in targeted neighbourhoods.

Preparation step. Identify specific neighbourhoods where you can demonstrate improved access to social infrastructure and services for homeless groups, and engage local stakeholders early so that the evidence your project generates is grounded in real territorial conditions, as the call explicitly requires evidence to be made available to stakeholders tackling homelessness.

Official topic page: HORIZON-NEB-2026-01-BUSINESS-01

Demonstrating place-based experimentation and innovation spaces as enabling environments for scaling EU Missions

Open, closes 13 January 2027. HORIZON-IA HORIZON Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: EUR 1.0 to 1.5 million per grant, about 3 grants expected (2026).

This call is designed as a strategic instrument for the second phase of the European Union Missions lifecycle, focused on scaling novel solutions rather than piloting them. It calls on territories including countries, regions, and interregional partnerships to design and demonstrate governance and regulatory experimentation environments. The expected outcomes include strengthening territorial anchoring of the Missions through multi-layered governance and generating best practices replicable across a wide array of European Union funding instruments.

Preparation step. Define the specific Mission or Missions your territory intends to support and articulate the governance and regulatory experimentation model you will demonstrate, since the description centres on designing and proving enabling environments for scaling rather than on producing new scientific knowledge.

Official topic page: HORIZON-MISS-2026-07-CROSS-04

Strengthening cybersecurity capacities of European SMEs with cybersecure AI-powered solutions

Open, closes 14 January 2027. DIGITAL-JU-SME DIGITAL JU SME Support Actions. Indicative budget: EUR 3.0 to 5.0 million per grant, about 5 grants expected (2026).

Small and medium-sized enterprises face growing cybersecurity threats but often lack the internal capacity to adopt and deploy sophisticated protective tools. This call under the Digital Europe programme focuses specifically on supporting the market uptake of automated cybersecurity solutions in smaller organisations, including tools and services that help prepare, protect, and respond to threats. It also explicitly covers employee training in the use of automated cybersecurity tools and the integration of protections for the automated solutions themselves.

Preparation step. Verify that the cybersecurity solutions you plan to deploy are market-ready and, where applicable, trace their lineage to earlier European Union-supported research and innovation projects, since the call description specifically encourages building on results from such projects as part of the market uptake and dissemination objective.

Official topic page: DIGITAL-ECCC-2027-DEPLOY-CYBER-11-AI4SME

Understanding the nature and the extent of the effects of PFAS on biodiversity and ecosystem services

Open, closes 14 January 2027. HORIZON-RIA HORIZON Research and Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: around EUR 5.7 million per grant, about 2 grants expected (2026).

Per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances, commonly known as PFAS, are a recognised flagship concern under the Water Resilience Strategy, and their effects on biodiversity and ecosystem services remain insufficiently understood at a mechanistic level. This call seeks to improve that mechanistic understanding, evaluate the extent of impacts, and identify remaining knowledge gaps so that policymakers can integrate findings into decisions on biodiversity protection, environmental protection, and human health. The expected outcomes also include supporting socio-economic cost-benefit analysis for policy measures on PFAS.

Preparation step. Structure your research design around mechanistic pathways, meaning the biological and ecological processes through which PFAS affect biodiversity and ecosystem services, because the description explicitly requires mechanistic understanding rather than purely observational or epidemiological approaches, and plan from the outset how findings will be framed for policy integration.

Official topic page: HORIZON-NATURE-2027-01-01
