EU Research Funding Calls Worth Preparing For

By the Cedulor team. As of 4 October 2026.

This is the edition of 4 October 2026. The dates and statuses below are as they stood on that day and may have changed since. Read the current edition.

The calls below span a wide range of research and innovation ambitions, from border security and bioenergy to social infrastructure, deep-tech scale-up, and frontier science. Cedulor has mapped each call to a concrete preparation step so that research offices and investigators can focus their effort where it matters most well before the deadline.

Advanced border surveillance and situational awareness

Open, closes 5 November 2026. HORIZON-IA HORIZON Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: around EUR 6.0 million per grant, about 2 grants expected (2026).

This Innovation Action targets a genuinely complex operational challenge: improving the detection, localization, characterization, and where appropriate neutralisation of unmanned vehicles, including swarms, involved in cross-border trafficking, while simultaneously strengthening situational awareness during large-scale population movements. The explicit requirement to comply with legal and ethical guidelines signals that proposals will be scrutinized not only on technical performance but on fundamental rights considerations. Reliable and redundant detection and reaction capacities that protect staff and society are a stated priority, which means solutions must demonstrate robustness under real-world conditions.

Preparation step. Map your consortium's technical capabilities against the full spectrum of scenarios described: land and sea borders, swarm detection, large-scale population movements, and fundamental rights compliance. Prepare a structured gap analysis identifying which scenarios your team covers well and which require additional partners or expertise, so you can build a coherent scope before drafting begins.

Official topic page: HORIZON-CL3-2026-01-BM-01

Demonstration of solid biofuel supply and conversion to high efficiency CHP from fully sustainable regional value chains

Open, closes 1 December 2026. HORIZON-IA HORIZON Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: around EUR 6.8 million per grant, about 2 grants expected (2026).

This Innovation Action calls for a full demonstration of regional value chains that source sustainable solid biofuel from upgraded biogenic residues and wastes and convert it in high-efficiency combined heat and power plants, all in line with the Renewable Energy Directive. The emphasis on regional value generation and opportunities for small and medium-sized enterprises makes it a strong fit for consortia that can link local biomass suppliers with industrial heat and power users. Flexible renewable power and heat delivery to local industries is highlighted as a concrete outcome, which means the demonstration must go beyond laboratory or pilot scale.

Preparation step. Identify a specific regional geography where biogenic residue streams are available and a credible combined heat and power installation site exists, then document the supply chain linkages and industrial heat demand in that region. This evidence of a real, mappable value chain will be essential for framing the demonstration as genuinely regional rather than generic.

Official topic page: HORIZON-CL5-2026-11-D3-05

Structurally addressing homelessness through coordinated social infrastructure and services in neighbourhoods

Open, closes 1 December 2026. HORIZON-IA HORIZON Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: around EUR 3.5 million per grant, about 3 grants expected (2026).

The call is grounded in concrete European data: over 1.2 million people experiencing homelessness, with 45 percent of surveyed individuals in chronic homelessness for more than two years. It targets the structural and multidimensional causes of homelessness at neighbourhood level, requiring systemic interventions that go beyond housing-led approaches alone. The expected outcomes ask for evidence of what social infrastructure and services are needed, as well as improved availability and accessibility for homeless groups in targeted neighbourhoods, which means both knowledge production and tangible local change are required.

Preparation step. Select and document the specific neighbourhoods where your project will intervene, establishing baseline data on the social infrastructure and services currently available to homeless groups there. Grounding the proposal in named, characterized locations with existing data will directly support the evidence-generation outcome that the call description requires.

Official topic page: HORIZON-NEB-2026-01-BUSINESS-01

EIC Accelerator 2026 - Short proposal

Open, closes 17 December 2026. HORIZON-EIC-ACC HORIZON EIC Accelerator.

The European Innovation Council Accelerator is designed specifically for small and medium-sized enterprises and start-ups that need a combination of grant and investment funding alongside Business Acceleration Services to scale innovations that would otherwise struggle to attract private financing. The focus on deep tech, meaning innovations built on scientific discovery or technological breakthroughs, and on patient capital situations where returns take a long time to materialise, sets a high bar for demonstrating both scientific depth and commercial ambition. The programme aims to shorten the time to full investment by providing blended finance, which means the proposal must articulate a credible scale-up pathway.

Preparation step. Before drafting the short proposal, prepare a clear articulation of why your innovation qualifies as deep tech and why the risk profile and time horizon make it unsuitable for conventional private investment alone. This framing is central to the Accelerator's rationale as described and will determine whether evaluators see the application as a genuine fit for blended finance support.

Official topic page: HORIZON-EIC-2026-ACCELERATOR-01

ERC Consolidator Grant

Open, closes 12 January 2027. HORIZON-ERC HORIZON ERC Grants.

The European Research Council Consolidator Grant is aimed at principal investigators who have already demonstrated research independence and are at the stage of consolidating their own team or programme, making the track record section of the proposal particularly consequential. Grants can reach substantial amounts over a sixty-month period, with the possibility of requesting additional funding for eligible costs that are necessary to carry out the proposed work, provided the justification is thorough. The pro rata temporis reduction for shorter durations means that the proposed timeline should be carefully calibrated to match the scientific ambition and the budget requested. Because the grant supports a single principal investigator's programme rather than a consortium, the intellectual coherence and originality of the research vision carry exceptional weight.

Preparation step. Before drafting the scientific proposal, prepare a structured account of your independent research contributions to date, since the description requires evidence of research independence as a condition of eligibility, and evaluators will assess whether the proposed programme represents a genuine consolidation of an original line of inquiry.

Official topic page: ERC-2027-COG
