EU Research Funding Calls Worth Preparing For

By the Cedulor team. As of 16 October 2026.

This is the edition of 16 October 2026. The dates and statuses below are as they stood on that day and may have changed since. Read the current edition.

The calls below span a wide arc of European research and innovation priorities, from doctoral training and frontier science to bioenergy demonstration, social infrastructure, and cybersecurity. Cedulor has surfaced each one with a tailored preparation step so that your team can move from initial assessment to a focused proposal plan well before the deadline.

MSCA Doctoral Networks 2026

Open, closes 24 November 2026. HORIZON-TMA-MSCA-DN HORIZON TMA MSCA Doctoral Networks.

Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions Doctoral Networks are a flagship mechanism for building structured, cross-sectoral doctoral training at European scale. This call explicitly targets improved transferable skills, enhanced employability both inside and outside academia, and boosted research and innovation capacity across participating organisations. The emphasis on knowledge transfer between sectors and disciplines makes it particularly relevant for consortia that span universities, research institutes, and industry partners. Internationalisation is also a stated outcome, signalling that the network's geographic and institutional breadth will be scrutinised.

Preparation step. Map your prospective consortium against the call's dual focus on cross-sector cooperation and internationalisation: document each partner's distinct disciplinary or sectoral contribution and identify any gaps in geographic spread or non-academic participation that should be filled before drafting the training programme.

Demonstration of solid biofuel supply and conversion to high efficiency CHP from fully sustainable regional value chains

Open, closes 1 December 2026. HORIZON-IA HORIZON Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: around EUR 6.8 million per grant, about 2 grants expected (2026).

This Innovation Action targets the full regional value chain for solid biofuel, from sustainably sourced biogenic residues and wastes through to high-efficiency combined heat and power generation, directly linking to the Renewable Energy Directive. The call frames success in terms of flexible renewable power and heat delivery to local industries, regional value generation, and European competitiveness in the sector. Demonstration at scale is the core requirement, making real-world site readiness and supply-chain traceability central concerns. Small and medium-sized enterprises are explicitly mentioned as beneficiaries, which shapes the expected consortium profile.

Preparation step. Secure a demonstration site with documented access to locally or regionally sourced biogenic residues and wastes, and prepare a preliminary sustainability assessment aligned with the Renewable Energy Directive provisions cited in the description, since compliance with those provisions is a stated condition for the value chain to qualify.

Official topic page: HORIZON-CL5-2026-11-D3-05

Structurally addressing homelessness through coordinated social infrastructure and services in neighbourhoods

Open, closes 1 December 2026. HORIZON-IA HORIZON Innovation Actions. Indicative budget: around EUR 3.5 million per grant, about 3 grants expected (2026).

This call responds to a concrete and large-scale social challenge: over 1.2 million people experiencing homelessness across Europe, with nearly half in chronic situations lasting more than two years. It goes beyond housing-led responses to require systemic, neighbourhood-level interventions that address structural and multidimensional causes. The expected outcomes demand both evidence generation and measurable improvements in the availability and accessibility of social infrastructure and services for homeless groups in targeted neighbourhoods. This dual requirement for rigorous evidence and practical service improvement makes it well suited to consortia combining social research capacity with operational service delivery experience.

Preparation step. Identify and formally engage neighbourhood-level stakeholders, including local authorities and service providers, in at least one targeted area where baseline data on social infrastructure gaps can be collected; the call specifically requires that evidence be made available to stakeholders involved in tackling homelessness, so establishing those relationships early is essential to the proposal's credibility.

Official topic page: HORIZON-NEB-2026-01-BUSINESS-01

ERC Consolidator Grant

Open, closes 12 January 2027. HORIZON-ERC HORIZON ERC Grants.

The European Research Council Consolidator Grant is designed for Principal Investigators who have already demonstrated research independence and are actively consolidating their own team or programme. The grant supports up to sixty months of frontier research, with provision for additional funding to cover costs that are necessary to carry out the proposed work when duly justified. The scale of support available makes this one of the most significant individual investigator grants in the European research landscape. The requirement for demonstrated independence means the Principal Investigator's track record is the central element the evaluation will scrutinise.

Preparation step. Prepare a structured account of the Principal Investigator's research independence, drawing on publications, supervised researchers, and any prior grant leadership, since the call explicitly requires evidence of independence as a qualifying condition; this narrative should be drafted and reviewed well before the deadline so it can be stress-tested against the stated scope.

Official topic page: ERC-2027-COG

Cybersecure tools, technologies and services relying on AI

Open, closes 14 January 2027. DIGITAL-JU-SIMPLE DIGITAL JU Simple Grants. Indicative budget: EUR 3.0 to 5.0 million per grant, about 4 grants expected (2026).

This call under the Digital Europe programme targets the deployment of automated and computationally driven cybersecurity tools and services specifically designed to support Cyber Hubs, CSIRTs, NCSCs, and NIS Single Points of Contact. It places particular weight on information sharing and collaboration across national and cross-border entities, supported by Cyber Threat Intelligence produced through automated tools. The call also calls for original European Cyber Threat Intelligence feeds or services, pointing to a gap in the current European cybersecurity ecosystem that proposals are expected to address. Compliance with EU legislation for secure automated solutions is stated as a requirement, not merely a preference.

Preparation step. Define the specific Cyber Threat Intelligence outputs your project will produce and identify which of the named beneficiary entities (Cyber Hubs, CSIRTs, NCSCs, NIS Single Points of Contact) will validate and use them, since the call frames deployment and uptake by those entities as a core expected outcome rather than a secondary consideration.

Official topic page: DIGITAL-ECCC-2027-DEPLOY-CYBER-11-CYBERAI
